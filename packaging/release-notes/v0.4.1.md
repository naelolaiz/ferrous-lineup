## New in the app

- **A user manual**, in English and Spanish, written for people who play rather than program. *Help > User manual* opens it in the app's language, and *Help > About* links it and its PDF.
  - English: https://naelolaiz.github.io/ferrous-lineup/manual/
  - Spanish: https://naelolaiz.github.io/ferrous-lineup/es/manual/
- **Real instrument sounds from the start.** The app offers to download the GeneralUser GS SoundFont as it opens, and the instruments play from it once it is there. The sound settings now survive updates.
- **New defaults.** The Library is in front, the channel rack no longer opens by itself, and until you pick a note naming, notes are named in solfège when the interface is in Spanish.
- **The chord sheet** fits more bars to a line, its zoom keys zoom it, and `%` repeats the whole bar before, as musicians read it.
- **Recordings** dropped on the window are imported, play from wherever playback starts, and get the key of their longest stretch, which is right more often.
- **The band:** the walking bass leads into the next chord by a semitone, and styles that hold chords, as a choir does, move without parallel fifths or octaves.
- **macOS:** `.lineup` files open from Finder.

## Fixes

- The recovery copy of unsaved work is written within half a minute, even when the window is left alone.
- *Edit style* copies the whole band the song plays, not only the drums.
- The piano roll spells a chord's notes as the chord does (C, Eb, Gb for C diminished), and a twelve-tone row's groups keep the row's note names.
- Lead sheets keep chord symbols in letters, as printed lead sheets do in every language.
- Key names on the bar numbers no longer run under the next bars.
- The row window reads notes typed in solfège.
- In Spanish: the toolbar fits on one row, sections and copies of styles get Spanish names, and the Sets window finds sets by their Spanish nicknames.
