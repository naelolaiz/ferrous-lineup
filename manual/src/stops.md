# Shots, holds, rests and pushes

A style plays the same groove bar after bar, and that is what makes it a
style. But listen to a real band: at the end of a phrase they all hit
one chord together and stop dead, or hold a chord out under the singer,
or drop out for a bar so the drummer or the soloist is alone, or come
in with the next chord a little before the beat. Arrangers write these
moments into the chart, and they are what make a band sound arranged
rather than looped. The chord sheet has a mark for each.

## Typing the marks

Dots after a harmony stop the band; carets before it bring it in early:

| Type | Mark | What the band does |
|---|---|---|
| `C.` | **shot** | strikes the chord once, short, and is silent until the next harmony |
| `C..` | **hold** | strikes it and holds it until the next harmony |
| `C...` | **rest** | is silent until the next harmony |
| `^C` | **push** | plays it an eighth note early, and holds it over the beat |
| `^^C` | **push** | plays it a sixteenth note early |

A push can be joined to a stop: `^G7.` is a shot an eighth note early.
Under each marked harmony, a word says what the band does there
(**shot**, **hold**, **rest**, **push**); point at it for the whole
sentence. `N.C.` keeps its own dots: they are part of its name.

Try it: press **Ctrl+N** and type these bars, one per bar:

```text
C   ^F   G7.   Am..   F G7...   ^^C   F G7   C
```

- bar 2: the band plays F on the "and" of beat 4 of bar 1, an eighth
  note early, and lets it ring over the bar line;
- bar 3: the whole band strikes G7 once, with a kick and a crash, and
  then nothing until bar 4;
- bar 4: Am struck and held for the whole bar;
- bar 5: F in the style for two beats, then silence where G7 is;
- bar 6: C a sixteenth note early;
- bars 7 and 8: the style again.

## Setting them from a menu

Right-click a bar and open **Band on G7** (there is one for each harmony
in the bar) to pick **Shot (.)**, **Hold (..)** or **Rest (...)**, and
**Push (^)** or **Push a sixteenth (^^)**. **As the style goes** and **On
the beat** take the marks off. Like every change on the sheet, the band
plays again, **Ctrl+Z** takes it back, and the marks are copied with the
bars, as text too.

## The music

**Shots.** A shot (also a hit, a stab or a kick) is a chord the whole
band plays at once, short and accented, followed by silence. Big band
charts are full of them, the drummer playing them with the band on the
crash cymbal and the bass drum. A run of shots, one at the start of each
bar or two, is called **stop-time**: the band plays only the hits and
leaves the rest of the bar to the singer, the soloist or a tap dancer.
The verses of Muddy Waters' *Hoochie Coochie Man* are a famous stop-time
groove. In Ferrous Lineup each player strikes the shot its own way, the
piano its chord, the bass its note, the guitar a strum, a little harder
than the style, and the drums play a kick and a crash. The harmony is
still there for whoever plays over the silence.

**Holds.** A hold is a chord struck and held: at the end of a phrase,
under the last word of a line, or to let a soloist play freely over one
harmony. It is not a fermata: a fermata stops the beat, and the band
waits for the conductor, while a hold lasts exactly until the next
harmony, and the beat goes on underneath.

**Rests.** A rest is the band dropping out, drums too. Arrangers call it
a **break**: in jazz the band stops for a bar or two and the soloist
plays alone, and in funk and hip hop, the bars where only the drummer
played gave the "breakbeat" its name. Write the drums' break yourself in
the piano roll, or play it with **You play** (see [Locking a track, and
playing your part](locks.md)).

**Pushes.** A push, or **anticipation**, is a chord played before the
beat it belongs to and held over it. Without it, the downbeat is the
strongest moment of the bar; with it, the chord comes early and beat 1
is silent, and the music leans forward. That is **syncopation**, an
accent off the beat, and pushes are everywhere in pop, rock, funk and
Latin music: in the salsa **tumbao**, the bass plays the next chord's
root on beat 4, held over the bar line. A push of a sixteenth note is
subtler, the pull of funk and fast Latin music.

The caret looks like the **marcato**, the accent musicians write over a
note to play it strongly, and a push is played with an accent.
