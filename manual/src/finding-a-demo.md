# Finding a demo

Ferrous Lineup comes with more than five hundred demos: folk songs and
dances from all over the world, tangos, jazz standards, spirituals,
ragtime, and orchestral music from Bach to Stravinsky. Each opens as a
song you can play, read and change: its chords on the chord sheet with
their numerals, its melody on its own channel, and a band playing it.
This part of the manual is a guide to listening to them.

## The Demos menu

**File > Demos** lists them all:

- **The starting song**: the beat and chords Ferrous Lineup opens with.
- **Songs**: more than 340 tunes with their melodies, sorted by genre:
  **Latin American folklore**, **Tango**, **Jazz**, **Classical and
  film**, **Balkan and Turkish**, **Country and folk** and more.
- **Styles**: each of the Library's 177 harmony styles as a short song,
  to hear what a style does before you use it (see [Styles and the
  Library](styles.md)).
- **Twelve-tone**: each row style as a short piece, playing the row
  forward, backwards, upside down and both (see [Beyond keys: sets,
  rows and micromodes](sets-rows-micromodes.md)).
- **Micromodes**: each micromode style as a short piece, and *Webern's
  trichords*, a famous row made of a single micromode.

Every list is in alphabetical order. Hold the pointer over a demo to
read about it: who wrote it and when, and what to listen for in its
harmony, its rhythm and its form.

## Searching

Type in **Search demos**, at the top of the menu, and the submenus give
way to one list of the demos that match:

![File > Demos with "zamba" typed in its search box: the style Zamba, then Zamba de mi esperanza, Zamba del telar, Zamba por vos, Alfonsina y el mar and Balderrama, each followed by "Songs ▸ Latin American folklore", and fainter, the styles Cueca and Marinera. The pointer is on Alfonsina y el mar, whose notes say who wrote it, why, and how its harmony moves.](images/demos-search.png)

A search looks at each demo's title, who wrote it and when, its feel
and genre, its key and its time signature, and last of all the notes
about it. Each word you type has to start a word there: **bach** finds
Bach, not Offenbach. Capitals and accents don't matter, so **fur elise**
finds *Für Elise*. Some searches to try:

| Type | To find |
|---|---|
| **gershwin** | everything by George Gershwin |
| **d minor** | the songs in D minor |
| **6/8** or **7/8** | the songs in that time signature |
| **tango**, **zamba**, **ragtime** | a genre or a dance |
| **neapolitan** | the demos whose notes mention a Neapolitan chord |

The demos whose title starts with what you typed come first; demos
where your words were found apart, or only in the notes, come last and
fainter. **Enter** opens the first one, and the arrow keys go through
the rest.

## Opening a demo

Clicking a demo opens it in place of your song (asking first, if your
song has unsaved changes) and plays it from the start. A song demo
opens with the chord sheet in front, and the Library selects the style
and the rhythm the demo is closest to. The title bar says **(demo,
read-only)**: change anything you like, and **Save** asks for a new
file, so the demo stays as it was (see [Saving, opening and
recovering](saving.md)).

## Ways to listen

A demo is a lesson that plays. Some ways to use one:

- **Follow the chord sheet.** The bar that plays is framed, and the
  numeral under each chord says what it does in the key (see [What
  chords do](chord-functions.md)). Coloured numerals mark the chords
  from outside the key (see [Chords from outside the
  key](outside-the-key.md)).
- **Watch the harmony keys.** The chord that sounds lights up, and the
  chords that often come next are framed: guess which one comes.
- **Open a part.** Double-click a clip in the playlist to see its notes
  in the piano roll, the melody, the bass or the piano's chords.
- **Slow it down and loop it.** The practice row plays a hard passage
  slower, and over and over (see [Practising](practising.md)).
- **Play along.** **You play** takes a player away from the band so you
  can take the part yourself (see [Locking a track, and playing your
  part](locks.md#you-play)).
- **Change its style.** Click a style in the Library and the band plays
  the same chords another way: a tango as a bossa nova, a folk song as
  a jazz waltz (see [Styles and the Library](styles.md)).
