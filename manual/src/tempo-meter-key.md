# Tempo, time signatures and key changes

How fast a song goes, how its beats are counted and which key it is in
can be set for the whole song, and changed partway through, as a score
changes them.

## Tempo

The **tempo** is how fast the beats go, in **beats per minute** (BPM):
at 60 BPM a beat lasts a second, at 120 half a second. Slow ballads sit
around 60 to 80, most pop between 90 and 130, dance music around 120 to
130, and fast swing or punk well over 160.

The **TEMPO** box in the toolbar is the tempo the song starts at: drag
the number up or down, or double-click it and type one. The chord sheet
shows it too, next to the key. To find the tempo of a song you hear,
click **Tap** on the chord sheet on every beat (see
[Practising](practising.md)).

### Changing tempo partway through

The **Tempo** strip, above the bar numbers in the playlist, draws the
tempo as a line: flat while it stays the same, a jump where it changes
at once, a slope where it changes gradually. Click it at a bar to open
its menu:

- **♩ =** the tempo from there on: drag it, or click it and type one.
  A new tempo adds a change at that bar.
- **Get there**: **At once**, as after a pause or at a new section, or
  gradually, **over 1 beat**, **over ½ bar**, **over 1 bar**, **over 2
  bars**, **over 4 bars** or **over 8 bars**.
- **Remove this change** takes it away.

At bar 1 the menu sets the tempo the song starts at.

The strip labels each change as a score does: "♩ = 90" for a sudden
one, "rit. → 60" for a gradual slowing down, a **ritardando**, and
"accel. → 140" for a gradual speeding up, an **accelerando**. Songs
often slow down at the very end, and a ritardando over the last two bars
makes an ending sound like one. Playing, and exporting a WAV or a MIDI
file, follow the changes.

## Time signatures

A **time signature** says how the beats are counted. The top number is
how many notes make a bar, the bottom one which note: **4** for quarter
notes, **8** for eighth notes, **2** for half notes. 4/4 is four quarter
notes to the bar; 3/4, three, the waltz; 6/8, six eighth notes felt as
two beats of three. [Rhythm and meter](rhythm-and-meter.md) explains
them, and the odd ones such as 5/4 and 7/8.

### The song's time signature

**Time**, in the harmony keys, is the song's time signature, with how
many steps a bar has next to it. Open it to choose another:

- one click for the usual ones: **2/4**, **3/4**, **4/4**, **5/4**,
  **3/8**, **5/8**, **6/8**, **7/8**, **9/8** and **12/8**;
- or **Any other**: the top number, the bottom one (**1**, **2**, **4**,
  **8** or **16**), and **Beats grouped as**, for meters whose beats
  are groups of two and three notes, such as 7/8 counted **2+2+3**.
  **Use 7/8** then sets it.

A new time signature for the whole song puts every bar's notes into a
bar of the new kind, and **each bar lasts as long as before**: a song at
130 BPM in 4/4 plays at 97.5 in 3/4, three beats in the time four took.
The tempo changes move with it. The options at the top of the list,
shared with the Library, say what else comes in:

- **Drums**: the drums of the Library's style for that meter, such as a
  waltz for 3/4. Off, the song keeps its own drums, in the new meter.
- **Chord and bass parts**: how that style plays the chords and the
  bass. Off, the song keeps its own, in the new meter.
- **Use style instruments** and **Use rhythm's swing**: the style's
  sounds and its swing.

The status bar says what was done, as in "The song now plays Waltz in
3/4, every bar squeezed or stretched into a 3/4 bar". Going back to the
song's first time signature gives back exactly the song you had, and
**Ctrl+Z** undoes it in one step.

### Changing time signature partway through

Right-click a bar number in the playlist: under **Time signature from
bar 5** is the same choice, for that bar and the ones after it. The
bars before stay as they are, and so do the style and the instruments.
**Remove this change** goes back to the time signature before it. The
bar numbers show the time signature at bar 1 and wherever it changes,
and the chord sheet writes it at the start of the bar.

## Key changes

Picking another key in the **Key** menus moves the whole song to it,
and a **key change** partway through names a new key from a bar on,
without moving any note: both are in [Keys and
modes](keys-and-modes.md#key-changes-in-a-song). The same right-click
menu on the bar numbers has **Key from bar 5**, and the bar numbers
write the key in blue where it changes.

## Inserting and deleting bars

The bar numbers' menu also has **Insert a bar before**, **Insert a bar
after** and **Delete this bar**, which move everything after them, as
on the chord sheet (see [The chord sheet](chord-sheet.md#changing-the-bars)).
