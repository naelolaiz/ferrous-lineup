# Music glossary

The words of music this manual uses, each with a short meaning and the
chapter that explains it with examples you can hear in the app.

| Term | Meaning | Explained in |
|---|---|---|
| **Accidental** | A sharp (♯), flat (♭) or natural (♮) before a note, raising or lowering it a semitone; a double sharp or double flat moves it two. | [Notes, intervals and scales](notes-and-scales.md#sharps-flats-and-spelling) |
| **Andalusian cadence** | Four chords walking down from a minor tonic, Am, G, F, E in A minor, the E major chord heard as home. The harmony of flamenco. | [Tango and Latin America](tango-and-latin-america.md#flamenco) |
| **Arpeggio** | The notes of a chord played one after another instead of together. | [Chords and their names](chords.md#how-the-app-hears-chords) |
| **Augmented second** | A step and a half between two neighbouring notes of a scale, such as B♭ to C♯ in the makam hicaz, or F to G♯ in A harmonic minor. | [Odd meters and the Balkans](odd-meters-and-balkans.md#makam-hicaz) |
| **Augmented triad** | Two major thirds stacked, such as C E G♯, written C+. | [Chords and their names](chords.md#triads) |
| **Backbeat** | The snare drum on the weak beats, 2 and 4: the pulse of rock, soul and funk. | [Rhythm and meter](rhythm-and-meter.md#strong-beats-weak-beats-and-syncopation) |
| **Bar** | A group of beats, the first the strongest; bar lines divide the music into bars. Also called a **measure**. | [Rhythm and meter](rhythm-and-meter.md#beats-and-bars) |
| **Beat** | The steady pulse you tap your foot to. | [Rhythm and meter](rhythm-and-meter.md#beats-and-bars) |
| **Blue notes** | Notes such as the flat third and the flat seventh, played against the major chords under them: the sound of the blues. | [Jazz, blues, gospel and pop](jazz-and-pop.md#the-blues) |
| **Borrowed chord** | A chord taken from the parallel key, such as A♭ (♭VI) in C major, from C minor. Also called **modal mixture** or **modal interchange**. | [Chords from outside the key](outside-the-key.md#borrowed-chords) |
| **Bossa nova** | The Brazilian song of the late 1950s: the samba slowed down and filled with jazz chords. | [Tango and Latin America](tango-and-latin-america.md#brazil) |
| **Break** | A bar or two where the band stops and a soloist plays alone. | [Shots, holds, rests and pushes](stops.md#the-music) |
| **Cadence** | The end of a phrase, where the harmony comes to rest: authentic (V to I), plagal (IV to I), half (stopping on V) or deceptive (V to vi). | [What chords do](chord-functions.md#cadences) |
| **Cadential six-four** | The tonic chord with its fifth in the bass, on the dominant's bass note just before V: C/G before G7 in C major. | [Folk music of South America, and the Misa Criolla](folk-and-misa-criolla.md#the-misa-criolla) |
| **Chacarera** | A quick folk dance from Santiago del Estero, in Argentina, in 6/8 and 3/4 at once. | [Folk music of South America, and the Misa Criolla](folk-and-misa-criolla.md#the-chacarera-and-its-cousins) |
| **Chord scale** | The scale a soloist plays over a chord: the chord's notes, and the notes of its key between them. | [The soloist](soloist.md#chord-scales) |
| **Chord symbol** | A chord's name written over the music, such as Am7 or G/B. | [Lead sheets](lead-sheets.md#reading-a-lead-sheet) |
| **Chorus** | In jazz, once through the whole form of a tune. In a pop song, the part that comes back after each verse. | [Song form and sections](song-form.md#chorus-means-once-through) |
| **Circle of fifths** | The twelve keys in a ring, each a fifth from the next, in the order of their key signatures. Chords whose roots fall a fifth go round it. | [What chords do](chord-functions.md#roots-that-fall-a-fifth) |
| **Clave** | A syncopated pattern of one or two bars that the whole band fits around, the backbone of Cuban music. | [Rhythm and meter](rhythm-and-meter.md#strong-beats-weak-beats-and-syncopation) |
| **Clef** | The sign at the start of a staff that says which line is which note: the treble clef for high parts, the bass clef for low ones. | [Lead sheets](lead-sheets.md#reading-a-lead-sheet) |
| **Close position** | A chord's notes as near together as they go, inside an octave, as horns play them under the lead. | [A bigger band and the horns](bigger-band.md#close-harmony) |
| **Comping** | The chords a pianist or a guitarist plays under the tune, and the rhythm they play them in. | [Styles and the Library](styles.md) |
| **Compound meter** | A meter whose beats divide into three eighths: 6/8, 9/8, 12/8. | [Rhythm and meter](rhythm-and-meter.md#two-and-three) |
| **Concert pitch** | The notes as they sound, as a piano plays them. | [Lead sheets](lead-sheets.md#for-b-e-and-f-instruments) |
| **Contrary motion** | Two parts moving in opposite directions, one up and one down. | [Voice leading](voice-leading.md#how-each-part-moves) |
| **Diminished seventh chord** | Four notes a minor third apart, such as B D F A♭, written B°7 or Bdim7. | [Chords and their names](chords.md#seventh-chords) |
| **Diminished triad** | Two minor thirds stacked, such as B D F, written B°. | [Chords and their names](chords.md#triads) |
| **Dominant** | The fifth note of the key, and the chord built on it, V: the chord of tension that pulls home. | [What chords do](chord-functions.md#home-away-and-tension) |
| **Dominant seventh chord** | A major triad with a minor seventh, such as G7 (G B D F), the dominant chord of C. | [Chords and their names](chords.md#seventh-chords) |
| **Dorian mode** | The natural minor scale with a raised sixth: D E F G A B C D. | [Keys and modes](keys-and-modes.md#the-modes) |
| **Enharmonic** | Two names for the same key of the piano, such as F♯ and G♭, or F♯ major and G♭ major. | [Keys and modes](keys-and-modes.md#keys-with-two-names) |
| **Fill** | A short figure at the end of a phrase, often on the drums, that leads into the next. | [Song form and sections](song-form.md#variations-a-and-b) |
| **Four-part writing** | Harmony for four voices, soprano, alto, tenor and bass, as in a hymn or a Bach chorale: where the rules of voice leading come from. | [Voice leading](voice-leading.md) |
| **Function** | What a chord does in the key: tonic is home, subdominant moves away, dominant is the tension that leads home. | [What chords do](chord-functions.md#home-away-and-tension) |
| **Ground bass** | A bass line that repeats from start to end while the music above it changes. | [The concert hall](concert-hall.md#baroque) |
| **Guide tones** | The third and the seventh of a chord, which say what kind of chord it is. | [The soloist](soloist.md#chord-tones-on-the-beat) |
| **Habanera** | A Cuban dance whose rhythm, long, short, long, long, crossed to Spain, the River Plate and Brazil. | [Tango and Latin America](tango-and-latin-america.md#the-habanera) |
| **Half-diminished seventh chord** | A diminished triad with a minor seventh, such as B D F A, written Bø7 or Bm7♭5. | [Chords and their names](chords.md#seventh-chords) |
| **Harmonic minor** | The natural minor scale with a raised seventh, the leading tone: A B C D E F G♯ A. | [Keys and modes](keys-and-modes.md#major-and-minor-keys) |
| **Hemiola** | Two bars of three beats heard as three of two, or 6/8 against 3/4, in the same six eighths. In Spain and Latin America, the **sesquiáltera**. | [Rhythm and meter](rhythm-and-meter.md#two-and-three) |
| **Hold** | A chord the band strikes and holds until the next harmony, while the beat goes on. | [Shots, holds, rests and pushes](stops.md#the-music) |
| **Huayno** | The dance of the Andes of Peru, Bolivia and northern Argentina, in a quick 2/4, often on the minor pentatonic scale. | [Folk music of South America, and the Misa Criolla](folk-and-misa-criolla.md#the-andes) |
| **Interval** | The distance between two notes, named by the letters it spans (a third, a fifth) and measured in semitones. | [Notes, intervals and scales](notes-and-scales.md#intervals) |
| **Inversion** | A chord with a note other than its root in the bass: the third in the first inversion, the fifth in the second, the seventh in the third. | [Chords and their names](chords.md#slash-chords-and-inversions) |
| **Key** | The scale and the home note a piece is built on, such as C major or A minor. | [Keys and modes](keys-and-modes.md#the-key-of-a-song) |
| **Key signature** | The sharps or flats written at the start of each staff, those of the key. | [Keys and modes](keys-and-modes.md#major-and-minor-keys) |
| **Lead sheet** | A page with a tune's melody and its chord symbols, what a band plays from. | [Lead sheets](lead-sheets.md) |
| **Leading tone** | The seventh note of the major scale, and of the harmonic minor, a semitone below the tonic, which pulls up to it: B in C major, G♯ in A minor. | [What chords do](chord-functions.md#home-away-and-tension) |
| **Leap** | A move of a fourth or more between two notes of a part. A move of a second is a step, and of a third a skip. | [Voice leading](voice-leading.md#how-each-part-moves) |
| **Major scale** | Seven notes, with semitones between the third and fourth and the seventh and eighth: C D E F G A B C. | [Notes, intervals and scales](notes-and-scales.md#scales) |
| **Marcato** | In tango, the accompaniment hitting every beat, short and hard. | [Tango and Latin America](tango-and-latin-america.md#tango) |
| **Melodic minor** | The natural minor scale with a raised sixth and seventh: A B C D E F♯ G♯ A. | [Keys and modes](keys-and-modes.md#major-and-minor-keys) |
| **Meter** | How the beats group into bars, in two, three or four, and how each beat divides, in two or in three. | [Rhythm and meter](rhythm-and-meter.md#beats-and-bars) |
| **Micromode** | One of Francisco Kröpfl's twelve groups of three notes, used as harmony and melody at once. | [Beyond keys](sets-rows-micromodes.md#micromodes) |
| **Milonga** | A quick, cheerful dance of the River Plate, in 2/4, on the rhythm 3 + 3 + 2; the tango's elder. | [Tango and Latin America](tango-and-latin-america.md#tango) |
| **Mixolydian mode** | The major scale with a flat seventh: G A B C D E F G. | [Keys and modes](keys-and-modes.md#the-modes) |
| **Mode** | The major scale started on another of its notes: from D, the Dorian mode; from E, the Phrygian; and so on. | [Keys and modes](keys-and-modes.md#the-modes) |
| **Modulation** | A change of key in a piece. | [Chords from outside the key](outside-the-key.md#pivot-chords-changing-key) |
| **Natural minor** | The minor scale with nothing raised: A B C D E F G A, the white keys from A. | [Keys and modes](keys-and-modes.md#major-and-minor-keys) |
| **Neapolitan sixth** | A major chord on the lowered second note of the key, usually with its third in the bass: D♭/F in C major, written N⁶. | [Chords from outside the key](outside-the-key.md#the-neapolitan-sixth) |
| **Neighbour note** | A note a step above or below a chord note, which goes back to it. | [The soloist](soloist.md#notes-between-the-beats) |
| **Ninth** | A note a ninth above a chord's root, stacked over its seventh; the **eleventh** and the **thirteenth** go on stacking thirds. | [Chords and their names](chords.md#ninths-elevenths-and-thirteenths) |
| **Octatonic scale** | Eight notes, a semitone and a whole tone in turn: C D♭ E♭ E F♯ G A B♭. | [The concert hall](concert-hall.md#debussy-and-stravinsky) |
| **Octave** | The distance from a note to the next one up with the same name, twelve semitones. | [Notes, intervals and scales](notes-and-scales.md#twelve-notes) |
| **Odd meter** | A meter that counts its eighths in twos and threes mixed, such as 3+2+2/8, so its beats are long and short. Turkish musicians call it **aksak**. | [Rhythm and meter](rhythm-and-meter.md#odd-meters) |
| **Parallel fifths** | Two parts a fifth apart that move to another fifth in the same direction; four-part writing avoids them, and parallel octaves too. | [Voice leading](voice-leading.md#no-parallel-fifths-or-octaves) |
| **Parallel keys** | A major and a minor key on the same tonic, such as C major and C minor. | [Chords from outside the key](outside-the-key.md#borrowed-chords) |
| **Passing note** | A note between two notes a third apart, passing from one to the other by step. | [The soloist](soloist.md#notes-between-the-beats) |
| **Pedal point** | A note held or repeated, usually in the bass, while the harmony changes around it. Also called a **pedal**. | [Odd meters and the Balkans](odd-meters-and-balkans.md#five-beats-in-the-concert-hall-and-in-jazz) |
| **Pentatonic scale** | A scale of five notes. The major pentatonic, C D E G A, has no semitones; the minor pentatonic has the same notes from A. | [Jazz, blues, gospel and pop](jazz-and-pop.md#spirituals-and-gospel) |
| **Phrygian mode** | The natural minor scale with a flat second: E F G A B C D E. | [Keys and modes](keys-and-modes.md#the-modes) |
| **Picardy third** | A major tonic chord at the end of a piece in a minor key. | [Chords from outside the key](outside-the-key.md#borrowed-chords) |
| **Pitch class** | A note's name in every octave at once: every C, high or low, is the pitch class C. | [Notes, intervals and scales](notes-and-scales.md#twelve-notes) |
| **Pitch-class set** | A group of pitch classes, named by its set class, such as 3-11 for every major and minor triad. | [Beyond keys](sets-rows-micromodes.md#pitch-class-sets) |
| **Pivot chord** | A chord that belongs to the old key and the new, through which a piece changes key: Am is vi in C major and ii in G major. | [Chords from outside the key](outside-the-key.md#pivot-chords-changing-key) |
| **Polychord** | Two chords sounding at once, one over the other, written with a vertical bar: Eb7\|Fb. | [Chords and their names](chords.md#two-chords-at-once) |
| **Prime form** | The most compact way to write a set class, counted from 0, such as [037] for the major and minor triads. | [Beyond keys](sets-rows-micromodes.md#pitch-class-sets) |
| **Push** | A chord played an eighth note early and held over the beat it belongs to. Also called an **anticipation**. | [Shots, holds, rests and pushes](stops.md#the-music) |
| **Ragtime** | The piano music of around 1900 that jazz grew out of: a steady left hand under a syncopated right hand. | [Jazz, blues, gospel and pop](jazz-and-pop.md#ragtime) |
| **Relative minor** | The minor key with the same notes as a major key, a minor third lower: A minor for C major, which is its **relative major**. | [Keys and modes](keys-and-modes.md#major-and-minor-keys) |
| **Rhythm changes** | The chords of Gershwin's *I Got Rhythm*, on which jazz musicians wrote more new tunes than on any chords but the blues. | [Jazz, blues, gospel and pop](jazz-and-pop.md#the-standards-and-the-big-bands) |
| **Roman numeral** | A chord named by the note of the key it is built on, upper case for major and lower case for minor: in C major, G7 is V7 and Am is vi. | [What chords do](chord-functions.md#roman-numerals) |
| **Root** | The note a chord is built on and named after. | [Chords and their names](chords.md) |
| **Scale** | The notes of a key in order, from the tonic up to the tonic an octave higher. | [Notes, intervals and scales](notes-and-scales.md#scales) |
| **Secondary dominant** | The dominant of a chord of the key other than the tonic, written V/V, V7/vi and so on: D7 in C major is V7/V. | [Chords from outside the key](outside-the-key.md#secondary-dominants) |
| **Semitone** | The smallest step on the piano, from one key to the next, white or black. Also called a **half step**; two make a whole tone. | [Notes, intervals and scales](notes-and-scales.md#twelve-notes) |
| **Set class** | All the pitch-class sets of the same shape, moved to other notes or turned upside down, named by a number such as 3-11. | [Beyond keys](sets-rows-micromodes.md#pitch-class-sets) |
| **Seventh chord** | A triad with another third on top, a seventh above the root. | [Chords and their names](chords.md#seventh-chords) |
| **Shot** | A chord the whole band strikes once, short, and then is silent until the next harmony. | [Shots, holds, rests and pushes](stops.md#the-music) |
| **Shuffle** | The blues' long-short feel on every beat, usually a full triplet. | [Swing and feel](swing.md#where-swing-doesnt-apply) |
| **Simple meter** | A meter whose beats divide into two: 2/4, 3/4, 4/4. | [Rhythm and meter](rhythm-and-meter.md#two-and-three) |
| **Slash chord** | A chord with a bass note after a slash, such as C/E: C major with E in the bass. | [Chords and their names](chords.md#slash-chords-and-inversions) |
| **Solfège** | Naming the notes Do, Re, Mi, Fa, Sol, La, Si instead of C, D, E, F, G, A, B. | [Notes, intervals and scales](notes-and-scales.md#letters-or-solfège) |
| **Subdominant** | The fourth note of the key, and the chord built on it, IV: the chord that moves away from home. | [What chords do](chord-functions.md#home-away-and-tension) |
| **Suspended chord** | A chord with a second (sus2) or a fourth (sus4) in place of its third. | [Chords and their names](chords.md#suspended-chords) |
| **Suspension** | A note held over from one chord into the next, where it clashes, and then falls a step. | [Voice leading](voice-leading.md#a-seventh-falls) |
| **Swing** | Playing the two eighth notes of each beat long-short instead of even. | [Swing and feel](swing.md) |
| **Syncopation** | An accent where the meter doesn't expect one: between the beats, or on a note held over a strong beat. | [Rhythm and meter](rhythm-and-meter.md#strong-beats-weak-beats-and-syncopation) |
| **Tango** | The music and dance of Buenos Aires and Montevideo, born around 1900 out of the habanera and the milonga, mostly in minor keys. | [Tango and Latin America](tango-and-latin-america.md#tango) |
| **Time signature** | Two numbers at the start of the music: how many notes to the bar, and which note. | [Rhythm and meter](rhythm-and-meter.md#beats-and-bars) |
| **Tonic** | The first note of the key, home, and the chord built on it, I. | [What chords do](chord-functions.md#home-away-and-tension) |
| **Tonicization** | Making a chord of the key sound like a tonic for a moment, with its own dominant, without changing key. | [Chords from outside the key](outside-the-key.md#secondary-dominants) |
| **Transposing instrument** | An instrument whose part is written in other notes than it sounds, such as the clarinet in B♭, the alto sax in E♭ or the horn in F. | [Lead sheets](lead-sheets.md#for-b-e-and-f-instruments) |
| **Tresillo** | Three notes spread over eight eighths as 3 + 3 + 2: the habanera with its two middle notes tied. | [Rhythm and meter](rhythm-and-meter.md#strong-beats-weak-beats-and-syncopation) |
| **Triad** | A chord of three notes: a root, a third and a fifth. | [Chords and their names](chords.md#triads) |
| **Triplet** | Three notes in the time of two. | [Rhythm and meter](rhythm-and-meter.md#note-values-and-steps) |
| **Tritone** | Six semitones, three whole tones, such as F to B: the interval at the heart of the dominant seventh. | [Notes, intervals and scales](notes-and-scales.md#intervals) |
| **Turnaround** | The chords at the end of a tune that lead back to its start, such as I, vi, ii, V. | [Song form and sections](song-form.md#the-intro-a-turnaround) |
| **Twelve-bar blues** | Three lines of four bars: the first on I, the second starting on IV and the third on V, each coming home to I. | [Jazz, blues, gospel and pop](jazz-and-pop.md#the-blues) |
| **Twelve-tone row** | An order of all twelve pitch classes, each once, that a piece is built from, forwards, backwards, upside down and both. Also called a **row**. | [Beyond keys](sets-rows-micromodes.md#twelve-tone-rows) |
| **Vamp** | A short harmony repeated before the tune comes in, setting up its sound. | [Song form and sections](song-form.md#the-intro-a-turnaround) |
| **Voice leading** | How each part of the harmony moves from one chord to the next. | [Voice leading](voice-leading.md) |
| **Walking bass** | A bass line with a note on every beat, stepping from chord to chord. | [Song form and sections](song-form.md#variations-a-and-b) |
| **Zamba** | The slow handkerchief dance of northern Argentina, in 6/8. | [Folk music of South America, and the Misa Criolla](folk-and-misa-criolla.md#zambas) |
| **Zwiefacher** | A Bavarian dance that changes between bars of three and bars of two, in an order the tune fixes. | [Odd meters and the Balkans](odd-meters-and-balkans.md#meters-that-change) |
