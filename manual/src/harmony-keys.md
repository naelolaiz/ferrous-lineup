# The harmony keys

Along the bottom of the window are the **harmony keys**: a big key for
each chord of the song's key, numbered I to vii in Roman numerals. Hold
one and its chord plays. They are a way to hear the chords of a key,
to find the chords of a song by ear, and to see, while a song plays,
which chord sounds and where it is likely to go next. **F8**, or **View
> Harmony keys**, hides them and shows them again.

## The keys

Each key shows the chord's **numeral**, its **name**, and what it does
in the key, in a word and a colour:

| Colour | Word | Chords in C major | What they do |
|---|---|---|---|
| blue | **tonic** | I (C), iii (Em), vi (Am) | home, or near it |
| green | **subdominant** | ii (Dm), IV (F) | away from home |
| orange | **dominant** | V (G), vii° (Bdim) | tension, pulling back home |

[What chords do: numerals and functions](chord-functions.md) explains
the numerals and the three functions.

**Hold a key** with the mouse, or press **1** to **7** on the computer
keyboard, to play its chord on the channel selected in the channel rack
("Hold a key or press 1 to 7 to play on "Lineup Osc""). While it sounds:

- its notes light up on the small keyboard at the right;
- the line of hints at the top of the window spells it out: "IV: F
  major (F A C), subdominant: away. Key 4.";
- the status bar names it: "F: F major, chord IV of C major.";
- the chords that often come next get a frame, the most usual one the
  brightest, and the line next to the keys names the move: "F → G: step
  up to the dominant".

While the music plays, the key of the chord you hear lights up, and the
chords that often follow it are framed, so you can watch a song's
harmony move.

## The controls at the left

- **Key**: the song's key note and scale. Picking another **moves the
  song** into it, as described in [Keys and modes](keys-and-modes.md).
  While the playhead is past a key change, *Now in A major* appears
  under it, and the keys are that key's chords.
- **Time**: the song's time signature (see [Tempo, time signatures and
  key changes](tempo-meter-key.md)), and how many steps a bar has.
- **Triads**, **7ths** and **9ths**: chords of three notes (every other
  note of the scale), four (with the seventh) or five (with the ninth).
  In C major, V is G with triads, G7 with sevenths and G9 with ninths.
- The hints list, **Detailed**, **Short** or **Minimal**: how much the
  hints explain, here and in the piano roll. **Detailed** gives whole
  sentences, **Short** the same in a few words, **Minimal** only the
  chord names.
- **🔍 Find the key**: listens to the song and says which key it is in
  (see [Keys and modes](keys-and-modes.md#finding-the-key-experimental)).

When the key is a pitch-class set or a twelve-tone row instead of a
major or minor scale, the keys play groups of its notes, and the buttons
choose how many (see [Beyond keys: sets, rows and
micromodes](sets-rows-micromodes.md)). When the key is a micromode, the
keys are its forms, named by their degree (T0, T2, T1I…), with **Near
home** and **All forms** to choose which (see [A micromode as the
key](sets-rows-micromodes.md#a-micromode-as-the-key)).

## Playing chords to the band

**MIDI > Accompaniment follows chords** turns a keyboard into an
arranger keyboard: while the music plays, the chord you hold on a MIDI
keyboard or the computer keyboard becomes the chord of the
accompaniment. The chords and the bass line keep their rhythm and move
to your chord from their next note. One key plays its major chord, two
keys a minor third apart its minor chord. The last chord stays until you
play another, and the written notes come back when playback stops; the
song itself does not change.
