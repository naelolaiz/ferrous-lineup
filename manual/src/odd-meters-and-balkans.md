# Odd meters and the Balkans

The dances of Bulgaria, Greece, Macedonia, Serbia, Romania and Turkey
count their beats in groups of two and three eighths mixed, long and
short (see [Rhythm and
meter](rhythm-and-meter.md#odd-meters)). They are in **Songs > Balkan and
Turkish**, and the Library has a style for each meter. Count along, a
number for every eighth, leaning on the first of each group.

## Dances in five, seven, nine and more

| Demo | Meter | Counted | Listen for |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Paidushko horo*, Bulgaria | 2+3/8 | 1 2, 1 2 3 | a quick beat and a slow one; strains that turn around A minor, ii, before coming home to G |
| *Tsakonikos*, Greece | 3+2/8 | 1 2 3, 1 2 | the paidushko's limp the other way round; the chord changes on the short beat |
| *Ajde Jano*, Serbia | 3+2+2/8 | 1 2 3, 1 2, 1 2 | the lesnoto, long, short, short; D minor resting on C major, ♭VII |
| *Jovano, Jovanke*, Macedonia | 3+2+2/8 | 1 2 3, 1 2, 1 2 | E Phrygian with a G♯, the sound of much Balkan music |
| *Samiotissa*, Greece | 3+2+2/8 | 1 2 3, 1 2, 1 2 | the kalamatianos, the Greek circle dance; G, C and D7, the singers in thirds |
| *Svatbarska rachenitsa*, Bulgaria | 2+2+3/8 | 1 2, 1 2, 1 2 3 | a wedding dance, quick, quick, slow; A minor turning to A major |
| *Daichovo horo*, Bulgaria | 2+2+2+3/8 | 1 2, 1 2, 1 2, 1 2 3 | nine eighths that are not three times three |
| *Gankino horo*, Bulgaria | 2+2+3+2+2/8 | 1 2, 1 2, 1 2 3, 1 2, 1 2 | eleven eighths, the long beat in the middle |
| *Bučimiš*, Bulgaria | 2+2+2+2+3+2+2/8 | seven beats, the fifth long | fifteen eighths to the bar |

Open *Ajde Jano* and watch the channel rack: each beat's steps are
shaded together, six, four and four (see the picture in [Rhythm and
meter](rhythm-and-meter.md#odd-meters)).

## Makam hicaz

Many of these tunes use a scale with a step and a half in it: A, B♭,
C♯, D, E, F, G. Turkish musicians call it the **makam hicaz**; it has
the notes of the Phrygian mode with a raised third, and its **augmented
second**, B♭ up to C♯, gives it its eastern colour. Listen for it in:

- *Entarisi ala benziyor*, a Turkish song from Istanbul, in the *raks
  aksağı*, 2+3+2+2/8;
- *Çayeli'nden öteye*, from Rize on the Black Sea, in the *devr-i
  hindi*, 3+2+2/8;
- *Geamparaua de la Murfatlar*, a Romanian *geampara*, 2+2+3/8, whose
  strains all end on B♭ falling to A;
- the fifth strain of *Svatbarska rachenitsa*.

In the Turkish songs, singers and players go in unison over a drum, and
the chords in the demos are added from the notes the tune leans on.

## Five beats in the concert hall and in jazz

| Demo | Meter | Listen for |
|---|---|---|
| *Take Five*, by Paul Desmond, for the Dave Brubeck Quartet (1959) | 3+2/4 | the piano's vamp, E♭ minor for three beats and B♭ minor 7 for two |
| *Mission: Impossible* theme, by Lalo Schifrin (1966) | 3+2/4 | the piano's ostinato, ten eighths split 3 + 3 + 2 + 2 |
| *Pathétique Symphony, Allegro con grazia*, by Tchaikovsky (1893) | 2+3/4 | a waltz in five, a short beat cutting in before every waltz step; in the trio, the double basses hold D from its first bar to its last, a **pedal point** |
| *Mândrele*, a Romanian dance | 3+2/4 | the Lydian mode, C with an F♯ |

## Meters that change

Some music changes meter as it goes:

![The chord sheet of the demo Süsserl, in B♭ major: bars 1 and 2 in 2/4, bar 3 in 3/4, bars 4 and 5 in 2/4, bar 6 in 3/4, and so on, the time signature written in orange where it changes; the chords B♭, F7 and E♭ (I, V7, IV)](images/susserl.png)

- *Süsserl*, a Bavarian **zwiefacher**: bars of three, danced with a
  waltz step, and bars of two, danced with a pivot, in an order the tune
  fixes, 2 2 3, 2 2 3, 2 2 3 3. The beat stays the same; only the bars
  change, so the band plays oom-pa in the bars of two and oom-pa-pa in
  the bars of three.
- *Promenade*, from Mussorgsky's *Pictures at an Exhibition* (1874):
  bars of five and of six quarter notes, phrased like speech.
- *The Rite of Spring, Ritual of Abduction*, by Stravinsky: 9/8, 6/8,
  7/8, 3/4, 2/4, and towards the end bars of 5/8, 3/8 and 2/8.
- *Desarma y sangra*, by Charly García: a ballad in 6/8, with whole
  passages in 3/4.

To change the meter partway through a song of your own, see [Changing
time signature partway
through](tempo-meter-key.md#changing-time-signature-partway-through).
