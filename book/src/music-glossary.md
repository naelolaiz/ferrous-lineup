# Music glossary

The band chapters use the words working musicians use. This page gives
each one a short meaning and the chapter that shows it at work. The
[Rust glossary](glossary.md) is separate: it explains the language, not
the music.

| Term | Meaning | Explained in |
|---|---|---|
| **Comping** | The chords a pianist or guitarist plays under the tune, and the rhythm and voicings they use. As a verb, to comp is to play those chords. | [The band plays the chord sheet](step-9/band.md) |
| **Voicing** | Which notes of a chord sound, in which octaves, and how they are spread. A good voicing keeps common notes and leaves the melody room. | [The band plays the chord sheet](step-9/band.md) |
| **Voice leading** | Moving each voice of a chord to the nearest voice of the next, so the harmony joins smoothly instead of jumping. | [The harmony keys](step-8/harmony-keys.md#playing-a-chord-voice-leading) |
| **Changes** | The chord progression of a song, bar by bar. "The changes" are what the chord sheet holds. | [The band plays the chord sheet](step-9/band.md) |
| **Head** | The written tune, played at the start and again after the solos. In a jazz performance, the head is the melody everyone knows. | [Song form](step-9/song-form.md) |
| **Chorus** | In jazz, one complete pass through the form, from the top of the chart to the last bar. In a pop song, the repeated part that comes after each verse. | [Song form](step-9/song-form.md) |
| **Turnaround** | The chords at the end of a section that lead back to its beginning, such as I–vi–ii–V. | [Song form](step-9/song-form.md) |
| **Walking bass** | A bass line of one note per beat: the root, notes of the chord, and a step into the next chord's root. | [The band plays the chord sheet](step-9/band.md) |
| **Slash chord** | A chord symbol with a bass note after a slash, such as `C/E`. The bass may be a note of the chord (an inversion) or a note outside it. | [Chord names](step-9/chord-names.md) |
| **Inversion** | A chord with a note other than its root in the bass. In a triad, the first inversion has the third lowest and the second the fifth; in a seventh chord, the third inversion has the seventh. | [Inversions](step-9/inversions.md) |
| **Figured bass** | A continuo shorthand: numbers above or below the bass note name the intervals to play above it, such as 6, 6/4 or 6/5. Ferrous Lineup writes the figures after the Roman numeral, as `I⁶` and `V⁶₅`. | [Inversions](step-9/inversions.md) |
| **Roman numeral** | A chord named by the degree of the key it is built on: upper case for major, lower case for minor. In C major, G7 is V7. | [Chords, and naming what you hear](step-3/chords.md) |
| **Enharmonic keys** | Two names for the same keys of the piano, such as F# major (six sharps) and G♭ major (six flats). They sound the same; the composer picks the name that fits the music around it. | [Keys with two names](step-9/before-the-release.md#keys-with-two-names) |
| **Function** | What a chord does in the key: tonic is home, subdominant is away, dominant is tension that pulls home. The harmony keys colour them blue, green and orange. | [The harmony keys](step-8/harmony-keys.md) |
| **Borrowed chord** | A chord taken from the parallel key, the major or minor on the same tonic, such as A♭ (♭VI) in C major, borrowed from C minor. Also called **modal mixture**. | [Borrowed chords](step-9/chord-functions.md#borrowed-chords-modal-mixture) |
| **Picardy third** | A major tonic chord at the end of a piece in a minor key: the major third borrowed from the major. | [Borrowed chords](step-9/chord-functions.md#borrowed-chords-modal-mixture) |
| **Secondary dominant** | The dominant of a chord of the key other than the tonic, written V/V ("five of five"), V7/vi and so on. D7 in C major is V7/V, the dominant of G. | [Secondary dominants](step-9/chord-functions.md#secondary-dominants-v-of-v) |
| **Tonicization** | Making a chord of the key sound for a moment like a tonic, by playing its own dominant or leading-tone chord before it, without changing key. | [Secondary dominants](step-9/chord-functions.md#secondary-dominants-v-of-v) |
| **Cadential six-four** | The tonic triad in second inversion over the dominant's bass, such as C/G before G7 in C major. It sounds like the dominant held back, and moves on to V. | [Secondary dominants](step-9/chord-functions.md#secondary-dominants-v-of-v) |
| **Neapolitan sixth** | A major chord on the lowered second degree, usually in first inversion: D♭/F in C major, written N⁶. It mostly goes to the dominant. | [The Neapolitan sixth](step-9/chord-functions.md#the-neapolitan-sixth) |
| **Pivot chord** | A chord that belongs to both the old and the new key in a modulation, heard in the first and then reread in the second: Am in C major is vi, and ii in G major. | [Pivot chords](step-9/chord-functions.md#pivot-chords-changing-key) |
| **Step, skip, leap** | How far one part moves: a second is a step, a third a skip, a fourth or more a leap. | [How each part moves](step-9/part-motion.md#steps-skips-and-leaps) |
| **Contrary motion** | Two parts moving opposite ways. Between the top line and the bass it keeps them two independent lines. | [How each part moves](step-9/part-motion.md#motion-between-two-parts) |
| **Oblique motion** | One part holds its note while the other moves. | [How each part moves](step-9/part-motion.md#motion-between-two-parts) |
| **Parallel motion** | Two parts moving the same way and keeping the same interval number, such as sixths going to sixths. Parallel fifths and octaves are what classical part writing avoids. | [How each part moves](step-9/part-motion.md#motion-between-two-parts) |
| **Close position** | A chord voiced with its notes as near together as they go, all within an octave, as a horn section plays it. | [A horn section](step-9/horn-section.md#close-harmony) |
| **Equalizer (EQ)** | A filter that turns ranges of frequencies up or down, such as a channel's lows, middle and highs. It changes a sound's colour, not its notes. | [EQ, compression and delay](step-9/channel-effects.md#the-equalizer-turning-frequencies-up-or-down) |
| **Compressor** | An effect that turns a sound down when it goes over a threshold, by a ratio, so a part plays at a steadier level. | [EQ, compression and delay](step-9/channel-effects.md#the-compressor-evening-out-how-loud-a-part-is) |
| **Delay** | An effect that plays a sound again a little later, and again, each echo quieter; in the app, in time with the song. | [EQ, compression and delay](step-9/channel-effects.md#the-delay-echoes-in-time-with-the-song) |
| **Polychord** | Two chords sounded at once, written one above the other, such as `Eb7` over `Fb`. The ear hears both chords, not one large chord. | [Hearing chords](step-9/chord-hearing.md) |
| **Pitch-class set** | A group of notes named by pitch class, ignoring octave and order. Every chord is one, but the term also covers groups that have no chord name. | [Twelve-tone rows and pitch-class sets](step-8/twelve-tone.md) |
| **Prime form** | The most compact transposition or inversion of a pitch-class set, used to compare sets. Forte's catalogue lists each set class with its prime form and interval vector. | [Twelve-tone rows and pitch-class sets](step-8/twelve-tone.md) |
| **Twelve-tone row** | An ordering of all twelve pitch classes used as the material of a piece. Its forms are prime, inversion, retrograde and retrograde inversion. | [Twelve-tone rows and pitch-class sets](step-8/twelve-tone.md) |
| **Micromode** | One of Francisco Kröpfl's twelve three-note groups, classified by interval content and heard as a colour. The app can use one as a scale or draw with it. | [Micromodes](step-8/micromodes.md) |
| **Swing** | Playing the second eighth note of each beat late, so pairs go long-short. A full swing, 100%, is a triplet: two thirds and one third. | [Swing](step-9/swing.md) |
| **Shot** | A short, accented chord hit by the whole band, followed by silence. Also called a stab, a hit or a kick. | [Shots, holds, rests and pushes](step-9/stops.md#shots) |
| **Push** | A chord struck before its beat and held over it, written `^F`; also called an anticipation. It makes the music lean forward. | [Shots, holds, rests and pushes](step-9/stops.md#pushes) |
| **Hold** | A chord struck and held until the next harmony, with the beat going on underneath. It is not a fermata. | [Shots, holds, rests and pushes](step-9/stops.md#holds) |
| **Rest** | The band dropping out for a bar or more; in jazz, a break. In the app a rest also leaves the drums silent. | [Shots, holds, rests and pushes](step-9/stops.md#rests) |
| **Concert pitch** | Music written as it sounds, "in C": what piano, guitar, bass, voice, flute and violin read. A song's concert key is the key the band hears. | [Lead sheets for B♭, E♭ and F instruments](step-9/transposing-instruments.md) |
| **Transposing instrument** | An instrument whose written notes are not the notes it sounds, named after the note a written C sounds: a trumpet in B♭, an alto saxophone in E♭, a horn in F. Its music is written higher by that distance, so a song in C is in D for a trumpet. | [Lead sheets for B♭, E♭ and F instruments](step-9/transposing-instruments.md) |
| **Solfège** | Naming the notes with syllables, Do Re Mi Fa Sol La Si, instead of the letters C D E F G A B, as in Spanish, Italian, French and Portuguese. A chord is named the same way: Am7 is Lam7. | [The app in Spanish, and notes in letters or solfège](step-9/languages.md#notes-named-do-re-mi) |
| **Fixed do** | Solfège in which Do is always C, whatever the key, as the app and Spanish and Italian schools use. In **movable do**, used by some ear-training methods, *do* is the tonic of the key. | [The app in Spanish, and notes in letters or solfège](step-9/languages.md#notes-named-do-re-mi) |
